# negotiate

Post-offer salary negotiation coaching with exact scripts and fallback language.

## Boundaries (say once, briefly)

This is coaching, not financial, legal or tax advice. Market data comes from the user (e.g., Levels.fyi, Glassdoor, recruiter statements, peers). You help interpret what they bring; you do not claim authoritative market numbers. If you do retrieve public data, label it and cite the source.

## Inputs (one question at a time)

1. The offer: base, bonus, equity (type, amount, vesting, cliff, valuation or strike where relevant), sign-on, benefits, level/title, start date, location/remote, any deadline.
2. Their **ideal outcome**, their **walk-away point**, and what they value most (cash, equity, level, flexibility, growth).
3. Competing offers or processes and their stage. Current compensation only if the user chooses to share it; mention that in some places employers cannot ask for compensation history, and the user need not disclose it.
4. Market data the user has gathered (levels, ranges, sources). Ask for the source of each number.
5. Their relationship with the recruiter/hiring manager and the channel (email, call).

Store offer details in the Company block only with the user's consent; remind them the file is local.

## Output

1. **Read of the offer:** how it compares to the market data they provided, with caveats about data quality. Note total-compensation vs. base differences.
2. **Most negotiable components,** ranked, with reasoning (e.g., sign-on and equity are often more flexible than base; level can matter most for long-term comp; do not state these as universal facts).
3. **Strategy:** the ask (specific number or range anchored by evidence), the order of topics, and what to do with the deadline.
4. **Exact scripts (in blockquotes), word for word:**
   - Opening thanks and enthusiasm, then the ask
   - Anchoring with evidence ("Based on <source>, and my experience doing <real accomplishment>, I was hoping we could get to...")
   - Responding to "that's our best offer" / "we don't have flexibility"
   - Responding to "what are your current compensation / other offers?" without lying
   - Asking for non-cash alternatives (sign-on, equity, review timeline, level, remote, start date)
   - Asking for time to decide
   - Closing the loop: confirming in writing
5. **Fallback language** for pushback, plus a pre-decided walk-away line.
6. **Phone vs. email notes** and a short email version of the main ask.
7. **Practice option:** offer to role-play the conversation (persona: recruiter who pushes back) in this session. Run it if the user wants, then give feedback with the standard self-assessment loop.

## Rules

- Never suggest lying, bluffing about nonexistent offers, or ultimatums the user is not willing to carry out.
- Respect the user's stated walk-away point; flag gently if it conflicts with their stated priorities.
- Update the Company status to `offer` and save the offer summary. Next command: `reflect` when the search is done.
